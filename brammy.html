About this project

Brammy is a 3D stealth horror game made as a school project. The game is set inside the actual Scalda building where we study, with every corridor, classroom, and stairwell modelled after the real thing. The goal is simple: sneak around, collect three key items, and use them to repair the exit door before Brammy catches you.

The enemy, Brammy, is inspired by our real teacher. He roams the building and hunts you by sight and sound. You can find a breaker box on the map to turn on the lights, which gives you a better view but also makes you more visible to Brammy. You carry one of each item at a time: the Button, the Wires, and the Engine. Each must be brought to and used on the door separately to complete the repair.

The game ships with two difficulty modes. Eclipse mode is the standard experience: three inventory slots, normal stamina, and a manageable Brammy. IntelliJ mode cranks up the pressure: Brammy is 25% faster, you have only one inventory slot, and your stamina and stamina recovery are both cut by 33%. Every step counts.

Key highlights

  • Map based on the real Scalda building where we study
  • Brammy is inspired by our actual teacher
  • 3 repair items: Button, Wires and Engine, each carried and used separately
  • Breaker box mechanic to toggle the lights
  • IntelliJ mode for a brutal one-slot, high-speed challenge
  • Collectible items that permanently affect Brammy or the player

My Contributions

Everything I built for Brammy

Brammy Entity

I built the entire Brammy character from scratch: his AI, pathfinding, sight cone, hearing radius, and all state transitions (idle, patrol, chase, catch). He is the heart of the game and everything that makes him scary came from my code.

All Player Logic

I coded all player systems: walking, sprinting with a stamina bar, looking direction, and the full inventory system. This includes picking up and dropping items, slot management, and the one-slot restriction that kicks in on IntelliJ mode.

All Items & Upgrades

I designed and implemented every collectible item and upgrade in the game. Each one has a unique effect that changes how you play or how dangerous Brammy is. See the full breakdown in the Items and Upgrades section below.

All Sounds

I handled the entire sound design for the game: ambient tension, footstep audio, Brammy's chase sounds, item pick-up effects, and UI feedback. Sound is a huge part of what makes Brammy feel threatening, and I built all of it.

Items & Upgrades

Every item I built and what it does

SQL Injection

Usable Item

Stops time for 5 seconds. Use it to freeze Brammy mid-chase and slip past him. A high-risk, high-reward escape when he is right behind you.

Struts2

Permanent Debuff

When collected, Brammy becomes 10% slower for the rest of the game. A meaningful advantage that makes every encounter slightly less lethal, if you can reach it safely.

White Brew

Permanent Buff

Permanently speeds the player up by 10%. Once grabbed, you are faster for the rest of the run, stacking your odds of outrunning Brammy when it matters most.

Screwdriver

Permanent Debuff on Brammy

Decreases Brammy's sight range by 20% permanently. He becomes noticeably less perceptive for the rest of the game, giving you more room to move in the open.

Scrum Bell

Key Item

The Scrum Bell is used to unlock the Button item. Without it, the Button cannot be obtained, making the Scrum Bell an indirect but essential step in completing the door repair.

Tech Stack

Tools and technologies behind Brammy

Godot 4

The game is built entirely in Godot Engine 4. The node-based scene system made it easy to structure the player, Brammy, items, and the map as independent, reusable components.

GDScript

All game logic is written in GDScript, Godot's Python-like scripting language. It kept iteration fast and made it easy to prototype and tweak Brammy's AI and player movement on the fly.

Level Design

The Scalda building layout was recreated in 3D. Walls, rooms, corridors, and the breaker box all needed careful placement to reflect the real building while still being fun to navigate.

Audio / Sound Design

Godot's AudioStreamPlayer nodes power all the in-game sound. Positional audio adds tension by letting the player hear Brammy's footsteps getting closer, a core part of the fear.

Git / GitHub

Version control throughout the entire project. We used branches to split work between team members, keeping the Brammy AI, player systems, and level design isolated until they were ready to merge.

Challenges & Learnings

Challenges I faced

  • Building Brammy's AI so he felt genuinely threatening without being unfair. The sight cone, hearing radius, and chase states all had to feel natural.
  • Balancing the stamina system so sprinting was useful but not spammable. Too fast and there is no tension, too slow and the game becomes frustrating.
  • Making the item and upgrade effects stack correctly across the whole session without causing bugs when multiple items were picked up.
  • Designing the inventory system to work identically in both difficulty modes, just with a different slot limit, without duplicating code.

What I learned

  • How to build a finite state machine for enemy AI: idle, patrol, alert, chase, and catch states each with clean transitions.
  • That sound design is not decoration, it is gameplay. Getting Brammy's audio right was as important as getting his movement right.
  • Designing game systems with difficulty modes in mind from the start is much cleaner than bolting them on later. The inventory slot limit taught me that.
  • Working in a team on a shared Godot project: coordinating scenes, avoiding merge conflicts, and keeping each system modular enough for others to use.
